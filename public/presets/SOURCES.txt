Baked preset data (pittsburgh, johnstown, ellicott) — sources and terms
=======================================================================

Each preset directory is generated by scripts/bake-presets.ts from these public-domain U.S. government sources:

  elevation.f32  USGS 3D Elevation Program (3DEP), via the National Map 3DEPElevation ImageServer.
                 Public domain. Hydro-conditioned (river channels burned) by the bake script.
  imagery.jpg    USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program (NAIP), via the USGS National Map
                 USGSNAIPPlus ImageServer. Public domain. 4096 x 4096, stitched from 2048 x 2048 exports.
  roads.json     U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line roads, via TIGERweb. Public domain.
  buildings.json Building footprints and heights from OpenStreetMap, fetched once by scripts/bake-buildings.ts from
                 the OSM API 0.6 /map endpoint. (c) OpenStreetMap contributors, Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0 —
                 https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright. ODbL permits use, redistribution and derived works in a
                 public repository, provided the source is attributed and any DERIVED DATABASE is offered under the
                 same licence: buildings.json is such a derived database and is offered under ODbL 1.0. The other
                 baked files are public-domain U.S. government data and are unaffected by this.
                 Heights are recorded with their provenance per building: a `height` tag (measured), a
                 `building:levels` floor count converted at the metres-per-storey fitted to the buildings that carry
                 both tags (about 3.2 m low-rise, 3.95 m high-rise), or — for the low-rise majority, roughly 94 % of
                 Pittsburgh's 45 000 footprints — an estimate from the building's class and footprint area. The
                 estimates are flagged 'estimated' in the data and must be described as estimates.
                 Where OpenStreetMap has mapped very few buildings (Johnstown: 16 per km2, versus 700 in Pittsburgh),
                 the gaps are filled from Microsoft's Global ML Building Footprints — machine-extracted from
                 satellite imagery, with a per-building height measured from stereo imagery. Also ODbL 1.0, so the
                 licence above is unchanged: https://github.com/microsoft/GlobalMLBuildingFootprints. A footprint is
                 added only where OpenStreetMap has none, so hand-mapped buildings are never replaced, and these
                 heights are flagged 'remote' in the data (measured remotely, not surveyed).
  meta.json      Scenario text and parameters. Historic stages and flows come from NOAA NWS (gauge PTTP1) and
                 USGS peak-flow records (Stonycreek River at Ferndale, 03040000; Patapsco River at Hollofield,
                 01589000). Johnstown's 1889 flood: Lake Conemaugh volume and drain time from Coleman, Kaktins &
                 Wojno (2016), "Dam-Breach hydrology of the Johnstown flood of 1889", Heliyon 2(6) e00120; the 1977
                 flood from USGS Open-File Report 78-963. Ellicott City's storms, watershed and branch peak flows:
                 USGS Fact Sheet 2021-3025.

Please credit the agencies when reusing the data: "Elevation: USGS 3DEP · Imagery: USDA NAIP via USGS The National
Map · Roads: U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line · Buildings © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL)" (also stored as
`attribution` in each meta.json).

Live areas loaded in the app are fetched at runtime and never stored in the repository. They use Esri World Imagery
(© Esri and its imagery providers, under Esri's terms) and, as fallbacks, Mapzen Terrarium elevation tiles (AWS Open
Data) and OpenStreetMap roads (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL).
