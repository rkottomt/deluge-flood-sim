Baked preset data — sources and terms
=====================================

Presets: pittsburgh, johnstown, ellicott, asheville, nashville, houston, boulder (plus the procedural `sandbox`,
which uses no external data).

Each preset directory is generated by scripts/bake-presets.ts from these public-domain U.S. government sources:

  elevation.f32  USGS 3D Elevation Program (3DEP), via the National Map 3DEPElevation ImageServer.
                 Public domain. Hydro-conditioned (river channels burned) by the bake script.
  imagery.jpg    USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program (NAIP), via the USGS National Map
                 USGSNAIPPlus ImageServer. Public domain. 4096 x 4096, stitched from 2048 x 2048 exports.
  imagery-detail.jpg
                 The close-up inset: a second NAIP photo (same ImageServer, same public-domain terms) covering only
                 the `imageryDetail` rectangle of meta.json - the middle of the domain, where the scenario cameras
                 go. 4096 x 4096, stitched from four 2048 x 2048 exports of that rectangle's quadrants, so it lands
                 on exactly the same ground as imagery.jpg (checked per preset: best-fit shift 0 px in both axes,
                 mean colour within 2 % per channel - artifacts/detail-imagery/inset-vs-base.mjs).
                 Baked where the base photo is coarser than NAIP itself resolves, which measurements over downtown
                 Pittsburgh (artifacts/detail-imagery) put just under 1 m/texel:

                     pittsburgh  3.0 km at 0.73 m/texel  (base 1.95)   asheville  3.0 km at 0.73 m/texel  (base 1.95)
                     johnstown   2.5 km at 0.61 m/texel  (base 1.71)   houston    3.0 km at 0.73 m/texel  (base 1.95)

                 ellicott and boulder have none on purpose: their 5 km domains already give 1.22 m/texel, close
                 enough to NAIP's own limit that an inset would add megabytes and no detail. Nashville (1.46 m/texel)
                 would gain from one, and it was baked and measured (2.4x, 3.8 MB), but public/presets is served
                 from a public static host under a 90 MB budget (tests/data/presets.test.ts) and five insets came to
                 91.5 MB - so the bytes went to the four blurriest domains instead.
  roads.json     U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line roads, via TIGERweb. Public domain.
  reference.json / reference.bin
                 pittsburgh only, and not third-party data: the grid-convergence study's 4096² run of this repo's
                 own solver on the 1936 crest, resampled to the shipped 1024² grid and quantised to one byte per
                 cell (scripts/reference-run.ts --export-overlay; format in ARCHITECTURE.md). The app draws it as the
                 "Reference (4096²)" overlay. Derived from the elevation above, so the same USGS terms apply.
  meta.json      Scenario text and parameters. Historic stages and flows come from NOAA NWS (gauge PTTP1) and
                 USGS peak-flow records (Stonycreek River at Ferndale, 03040000; Patapsco River at Hollofield,
                 01589000). Johnstown's 1889 flood: Lake Conemaugh volume and drain time from Coleman, Kaktins &
                 Wojno (2016), "Dam-Breach hydrology of the Johnstown flood of 1889", Heliyon 2(6) e00120; the 1977
                 flood from USGS Open-File Report 78-963. Ellicott City's storms, watershed and branch peak flows:
                 USGS Fact Sheet 2021-3025.

Scenario provenance for the 2024/2025 additions
-----------------------------------------------

River centerlines for these four are traced from USGS National Hydrography Dataset high-resolution flowlines
(hydro.nationalmap.gov, layer "Flowline - Large Scale"; public domain), clipped to each domain — see
artifacts/more-cities/nhd_path.py. Peak discharges and stages are from the USGS annual peak-flow files
(nwis.waterdata.usgs.gov/nwis/peak) and gauge datums from the NWIS expanded site file; both public domain.

  asheville   Hurricane Helene, September 27, 2024. French Broad River at Asheville (USGS 03451500): peak
              113,000 ft³/s at 24.82 ft, beating 110,000 ft³/s at 23.10 ft on July 16, 1916. Swannanoa River at
              Biltmore (USGS 03451000): 60,800 ft³/s at 27.33 ft, beating 23,000 ft³/s in 1916. Gauge datum
              1,949.93 ft NAVD88 (03451500). Damage to the River Arts District and Biltmore Village and the loss of
              the city's water supply until November: NC/Asheville reporting and City of Asheville recovery records.

  nashville   May 2010 flood, with the pre-dam record above it. Cumberland River at Nashville (USGS 03431500):
              gauge datum 367.45 ft NAVD88, NWS flood stage 40 ft. The three crests on the stage slider are the
              gauge's own annual peaks, and the NWS Nashville crest table agrees with each: 203,000 ft³/s at
              56.20 ft (January 1, 1927 — the record), 186,000 ft³/s at 53.90 ft (January 26, 1937), both before
              the Corps of Engineers dams, and 188,000 ft³/s at 52.55 ft (May 3, 2010), the highest since. The
              one-click flood raises the river to the highest mark it can reach, so it plays 1927; 2010 and 1937
              are on the jump row beside it. 52.55 ft is the crest both USGS and NWS record for 2010 — the
              51.86 ft this file used to give was the preliminary reading published while the river was still
              rising. The 13.57 in of rain on May 1–2, 2010 (against a previous two-day record of 6.68 in),
              18 deaths in Middle Tennessee and >$2 billion of damage: NWS Nashville (weather.gov/ohx).

  houston     Hurricane Harvey, August 2017. Buffalo Bayou at Houston (USGS 08074000, Shepherd Drive): peak
              32,600 ft³/s at 41.90 ft on August 28, 2017; 1935 peak 40,000 ft³/s at 49.00 ft. Rainfall — 60.58 in
              at Nederland (a US tropical-cyclone record), >40 in over much of Harris County, and 6.8 in in one hour
              over southeastern Houston, the rate this scenario rains at: NOAA National Hurricane Center Tropical
              Cyclone Report AL092017 (Blake & Zelinsky). Addicks and Barker reservoir releases: USACE Galveston District.

  boulder     September 2013 Front Range flood. Boulder Creek at North 75th St near Boulder (USGS 06730200): peak
              8,400 ft³/s at 10.60 ft on September 13, 2013, against a previous record of 2,050 ft³/s (2003).
              Rainfall — 17.15 in over Boulder County September 9–16 and a record 9.08 in on September 12, the rate
              this scenario rains at: NWS Boulder (weather.gov/bou/Number1September2013Floods).

Place names used for shelters and camera framing are ordinary geographic names; no third-party place database is
redistributed here.

Please credit the agencies when reusing the data: "Elevation: USGS 3DEP · Imagery: USDA NAIP via USGS The National
Map · Roads: U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line" (also stored as `attribution` in each meta.json).

Live areas loaded in the app are fetched at runtime and never stored in the repository. They use Esri World Imagery
(© Esri and its imagery providers, under Esri's terms) and, as fallbacks, Mapzen Terrarium elevation tiles (AWS Open
Data) and OpenStreetMap roads (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL).
