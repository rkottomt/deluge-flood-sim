Baked preset data — sources and terms
=====================================

Presets: pittsburgh, johnstown, ellicott, asheville, nashville, houston, boulder, ftmyers, nepal (plus the
procedural `sandbox`, which uses no external data).

The first eight are U.S. domains. NEPAL IS NOT: it is built from a different set of services under different licences,
and its own section at the bottom of this file covers all of them. Nothing in the list immediately below applies to it.

Each U.S. preset directory is generated by scripts/bake-presets.ts from these public-domain U.S. government sources:

  elevation.f32  USGS 3D Elevation Program (3DEP), via the National Map 3DEPElevation ImageServer.
                 Public domain. Hydro-conditioned (river channels burned) by the bake script.
  imagery.jpg    USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program (NAIP), via the USGS National Map
                 USGSNAIPPlus ImageServer. Public domain. 4096 x 4096, stitched from 2048 x 2048 exports.
  imagery-detail.jpg
                 The close-up inset: a second NAIP photo (same ImageServer, same public-domain terms) covering only
                 the `imageryDetail` rectangle of meta.json - the middle of the domain, where the scenario cameras
                 go. 4096 x 4096, stitched from four 2048 x 2048 exports of that rectangle's quadrants, so it lands
                 on exactly the same ground as imagery.jpg (checked per preset: best-fit shift 0 px in both axes,
                 mean colour within 2 % per channel - artifacts/detail-imagery/inset-vs-base.mjs).
                 Baked where the base photo is coarser than NAIP itself resolves, which measurements over downtown
                 Pittsburgh (artifacts/detail-imagery) put just under 1 m/texel:

                     pittsburgh  3.0 km at 0.73 m/texel  (base 1.95)   asheville  3.0 km at 0.73 m/texel  (base 1.95)
                     johnstown   2.5 km at 0.61 m/texel  (base 1.71)   houston    3.0 km at 0.73 m/texel  (base 1.95)

                 ellicott and boulder have none on purpose: their 5 km domains already give 1.22 m/texel, close
                 enough to NAIP's own limit that an inset would add megabytes and no detail. Nashville (1.46 m/texel)
                 would gain from one, and it was baked and measured (2.4x, 3.8 MB), but public/presets is served
                 from a public static host under a 90 MB budget (tests/data/presets.test.ts) and five insets came to
                 91.5 MB - so the bytes went to the four blurriest domains instead.
  roads.json     U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line roads, via TIGERweb. Public domain.
  meta.json      Scenario text and parameters. Historic stages and flows come from NOAA NWS (gauge PTTP1) and
                 USGS peak-flow records (Stonycreek River at Ferndale, 03040000; Patapsco River at Hollofield,
                 01589000). Johnstown's 1889 flood: Lake Conemaugh volume and drain time from Coleman, Kaktins &
                 Wojno (2016), "Dam-Breach hydrology of the Johnstown flood of 1889", Heliyon 2(6) e00120; the 1977
                 flood from USGS Open-File Report 78-963. Ellicott City's storms, watershed and branch peak flows:
                 USGS Fact Sheet 2021-3025.

Scenario provenance for the 2024/2025 additions
-----------------------------------------------

River centerlines for these four are traced from USGS National Hydrography Dataset high-resolution flowlines
(hydro.nationalmap.gov, layer "Flowline - Large Scale"; public domain), clipped to each domain — see
artifacts/more-cities/nhd_path.py. Peak discharges and stages are from the USGS annual peak-flow files
(nwis.waterdata.usgs.gov/nwis/peak) and gauge datums from the NWIS expanded site file; both public domain.

  asheville   Hurricane Helene, September 27, 2024. French Broad River at Asheville (USGS 03451500): peak
              113,000 ft³/s at 24.82 ft, beating 110,000 ft³/s at 23.10 ft on July 16, 1916. Swannanoa River at
              Biltmore (USGS 03451000): 60,800 ft³/s at 27.33 ft, beating 23,000 ft³/s in 1916. Gauge datum
              1,949.93 ft NAVD88 (03451500). Damage to the River Arts District and Biltmore Village and the loss of
              the city's water supply until November: NC/Asheville reporting and City of Asheville recovery records.

  nashville   May 2010 flood. Cumberland River at Nashville (USGS 03431500): gauge datum 367.45 ft NAVD88; peak
              188,000 ft³/s at 52.55 ft on May 3, 2010; earlier peaks 203,000 ft³/s at 56.20 ft (January 1927) and
              186,000 ft³/s at 53.90 ft (January 1937), both before the Corps of Engineers dams. Official NWS crest
              51.86 ft and flood stage 40 ft; the 13.57 in of rain on May 1–2, 2010 (against a previous two-day
              record of 6.68 in), 18 deaths in Middle Tennessee and >$2 billion of damage: NWS Nashville (weather.gov/ohx).

  houston     Hurricane Harvey, August 2017. Buffalo Bayou at Houston (USGS 08074000, Shepherd Drive): peak
              32,600 ft³/s at 41.90 ft on August 28, 2017; 1935 peak 40,000 ft³/s at 49.00 ft. Rainfall — 60.58 in
              at Nederland (a US tropical-cyclone record), >40 in over much of Harris County, and 6.8 in in one hour
              over southeastern Houston, the rate this scenario rains at: NOAA National Hurricane Center Tropical
              Cyclone Report AL092017 (Blake & Zelinsky). Addicks and Barker reservoir releases: USACE Galveston District.

  boulder     September 2013 Front Range flood. Boulder Creek at North 75th St near Boulder (USGS 06730200): peak
              8,400 ft³/s at 10.60 ft on September 13, 2013, against a previous record of 2,050 ft³/s (2003).
              Rainfall — 17.15 in over Boulder County September 9–16 and a record 9.08 in on September 12, the rate
              this scenario rains at: NWS Boulder (weather.gov/bou/Number1September2013Floods).

  ftmyers     Hurricane Ian, September 28, 2022 — coastal storm surge up the Caloosahatchee. Tidal datums and water
              levels from NOAA CO-OPS station 8725520 (Fort Myers, FL), 1983-2001 epoch, in feet above STATION DATUM:
              MHHW 5.68, MSL 4.99, MLLW 4.36, NAVD88 5.40 (api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov mdapi station datums). The
              station datum therefore lies 5.40 ft = 1.646 m below NAVD88, which is the gauge datum this scenario
              uses, so the stage readout in the app is the gauge's own reading. MHHW is +0.085 m NAVD88.
              The station's record water level, 12.923 ft = 2.293 m NAVD88, was set by Ian at 22:18 GMT on
              2022-09-28 (NOAA CO-OPS 6-minute water_level series, NAVD datum, 2022-09-28/29) — the 12.9 ft mark on
              the stage slider. No casualty or damage figures are used in this scenario's text.
              The DEM's own flat estuary surface measures -0.28 m NAVD88, essentially MLLW (-0.317 m): the 3DEP
              lidar was flown near low tide, and the bake measures that surface rather than assuming it.
              Shelter locations are high ground found in the DEM (artifacts/florida-build/probe-layout.ts) and named
              for the street at that point, confirmed by OpenStreetMap Nominatim reverse geocoding
              (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL) — used to NAME the points only; no OSM data is redistributed here.

Nepal — betrawati (the `nepal` preset). Sources and terms
---------------------------------------------------------

The only non-U.S. domain, and the only one whose forcing is a MODELLED scenario rather than a gauge record. All web
sources accessed 19-20 September 2026. The full research trail is artifacts/nepal-research/BRIEF.md.

  elevation.f32  Copernicus DEM GLO-30, tiles Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N27_00_E085_00_DEM and
                 ..._N28_00_E085_00_DEM, read directly from the AWS Open Data bucket
                 https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com (no requester-pays) by src/data/demGlobal.ts.
                 (c) DLR e.V. 2010-2014 and (c) Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014-2018, provided under the
                 Copernicus DEM licence, which permits use and redistribution WITH ATTRIBUTION - the required credit
                 is carried in every meta.json as `attribution` and shown in the app.
                 1 arc-second posting (3600 samples per degree), PixelIsPoint, heights EGM2008 orthometric. The datum
                 was re-checked rather than assumed: against 3DEP (NAVD88, also orthometric) over four U.S. domains
                 the median difference is +1.2 to +3.9 m, where a WGS84 ELLIPSOIDAL height would have been about
                 -33 m at Pittsburgh and -17 m at Boulder (artifacts/nepal-build/out-datum2.txt).
                 The domain straddles 28 N, so the grid is a two-tile mosaic; the row holding 28.0 N differs from its
                 neighbour by less than the terrain's own row-to-row roughness (asserted in tests/data/demGlobal.test.ts).

                 IT IS A SURFACE MODEL. GLO-30 is radar, and canopy and buildings are part of its terrain. Without a
                 correction a flood runs over tree tops, so the bake applies a progressive morphological filter with a
                 20-degree slope gate (src/data/demGlobal.ts, bareEarthFromSurface). WHAT IT IS WORTH, measured
                 against USGS 3DEP bare-earth lidar on four U.S. domains at the same 7.81 m cell size
                 (artifacts/nepal-build/validate-global.ts bareearth, out-bareearth-padded.txt), on valley-floor cells
                 - the ground a flood actually spreads over - as filtered minus lidar:

                     Nashville (flat, urban)       raw +2.26 m mean, MAE 2.76  ->  filtered +2.04 m, MAE 2.57
                     Pittsburgh (steep, urban)     raw +4.01 m mean, MAE 5.24  ->  filtered +3.55 m, MAE 4.79
                     Asheville (steep, forested)   raw +2.11 m mean, MAE 2.22  ->  filtered +2.02 m, MAE 2.18
                     Boulder (steep, sparse trees) raw +3.03 m mean, MAE 3.04  ->  filtered +3.01 m, MAE 3.02

                 Read that honestly: the filter is an IMPROVEMENT, NOT A FIX. It takes buildings and small stands of
                 trees down, and it leaves a positive bias on the valley floor of one to three metres at the median
                 (two to three and a half at the mean, which the tail of remaining canopy drags up),
                 because a 30 m posting smears a building over several cells and because nothing in radar reveals the
                 ground under continuous canopy wider than the filter's 120 m window. On the Betrawati domain, which is
                 77 % steeper than 20 degrees, the slope gate leaves most of the valley walls as the raw surface model
                 on purpose: on that ground no filter can tell a roof from a spur nose, and shaving 8-12 m off real
                 spur crests (which the ungated filter did) would be worse than admitting the canopy. 2.5 % of the
                 domain's cells were adjusted, by 3.5 m on average and 12 m at most; the numbers for the shipped bake
                 are in meta.json under `bake.bareEarth`. THE SCENARIO TEXT ON SCREEN CARRIES ALL OF THAT, INCLUDING
                 THE SPLIT: that the filter only runs on ground flatter than 20 degrees, that 77 % of this domain is
                 steeper and ships as the raw surface model, that the filtered valley floor is still a metre or two
                 high, and that street-level depths must not be read from it. (Until the 19-20 September 2026 audit
                 the on-screen sentence said only "filtered towards bare earth", which reads as the whole terrain; the
                 77 % lived in this file alone. A caveat a visitor never sees is not a caveat.)

  imagery        NONE - half a licence decision and half an admission, and the two halves are different sources.
                 Sentinel-2 L2A COGs on AWS Open Data (https://sentinel-cogs.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com) are open
                 under the Copernicus licence and COULD be composed to RGB and redistributed with attribution;
                 composing a cloud-free August mosaic over a monsoon-season Himalayan valley was not something this
                 bake could do well in the time available, and a bad photo is worse than none. That half is a limit of
                 this bake, not of the licence. The EOX s2cloudless mosaics that would have been easy ARE a licence
                 decision: CC BY-NC-SA for 2018 and later, so they must NOT be baked into a redistributable
                 repository. Esri World Imagery is never baked for any preset (its terms do not allow it); the app
                 uses it only for live, runtime-fetched areas. The preset therefore ships hypsometric relief plus
                 roads, and `files.imagery` is null.
                 THE ON-SCREEN SENTENCE SAYS BOTH HALVES. Until the September 2026 audit it said "there is no aerial
                 photograph here that this project may redistribute", which flatly contradicted the paragraph above:
                 redistributable imagery of this valley does exist and was not composed.

  roads.json     OpenStreetMap, (c) OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Downloaded from the OSM API 0.6 `map` endpoint
                 (https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/map) in four quadrants, because the API refuses a box this
                 size in one request (artifacts/nepal-build/fetch-osm.mjs); 351 drivable ways became 464 edges and
                 202 km of road. Rasuwa and Nuwakot were remapped after the flood by the HOT/NAXA/NDRRMA campaign
                 (wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Organised_Editing/Activities/Nepal_Floods_2026), so the network is
                 post-event. River centrelines are traced from the same extracts: OSM ways 343007937 + 343007938 +
                 27033466 (Trishuli), 298744777 (Salankhu Khola) and 300686595 (Phalakhu Khola).

  meta.json      THE SCENARIO IS A SCENARIO. The event: on 26 August 2026 at about 08:37 NPT a rock-and-ice slope
                 failure on the north face of Langtang Lirung fell into the Lhende Khola and became a debris flood
                 down the Bhote Koshi and the Trishuli (ICIMOD, Kyirong-Rasuwa Flood 2026,
                 icimod.org/kyirong-rasuwa-flood-2026-nepal-china-border). It was NOT a glacial lake outburst flood
                 and it did not start in Tibet - early reporting that said either has been superseded by ICIMOD's
                 analysis, and neither claim appears in this app.
                 Numbers used on screen, each as published:
                   * about 20 million m3 of excess water down the Bhote Koshi / Trishuli / Narayani system - DHM Flood
                     Forecasting Division technical report, 27 August 2026. The inflow this scenario uses, 11,100 m3/s,
                     is that volume divided by 30 minutes. IT IS ARITHMETIC ON A REPORTED VOLUME, NOT A MEASURED PEAK,
                     and the app's own label and scenario text say so.
                     AND THE INFLOW STOPS AFTER THOSE 30 MINUTES (WaterSource.stopAfter, honoured by the solver), so
                     the run delivers 11,100 x 1,800 s = 19.98 million m3 - the reported total, to within the rounding
                     of the discharge to a hundred m3/s - and then nothing. It did not, until the September 2026 audit:
                     the source had no duration, so the same peak kept arriving and the run had put 2.07e7 m3 into the
                     valley by 31 simulated minutes and 5.15e7 m3 by 78, while the label still said 20 million.
                     tests/data/presets.test.ts asserts the product and tests/sim asserts the solver delivers it.
                   * Trishuli at Betrawati, DHM station 447 (dhm.gov.np/hydrology/hms-Single/52): warning level 4.1 m,
                     danger level 5.0 m, last reading 3.55 m at 09:20 before the station was destroyed. There is no
                     measured peak level or discharge here, and none is shown.
                   * the only measured peak discharge in the system, 5,850 m3/s at Devghat (DHM, same report), is
                     168 km downstream and is not used as a Betrawati figure.
                 NOT CLAIMED: that this reproduces the real flood. The solver is clear water at up to 15 m/s against
                 the roughly 20 m/s ICIMOD measured between Rasuwagadhi and Betrawati, on interpolated 30 m terrain.
                 Arrival times, depths and extent are model output, not measurements. The published delineation of the
                 real flood is Copernicus EMS activation EMSR927 (mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/news/flood-in-nepal-emsr927)
                 - the thing to compare against, explicitly, if anyone ever wants to.
                 SHARPER THAN THAT, AND NOW ON SCREEN: the arrival times in this run are SET BY the 15 m/s cap, not by
                 the terrain. Measured on the shipped bake, the reported maximum speed is already 15.0 m/s at 32
                 simulated seconds and stays there for the rest of the run (artifacts/nepal-verify/nepal-run.json), so
                 the front travels at the solver's ceiling from the first half-minute on and "when the water reaches
                 X" is a statement about the cap. What the model is for is the ORDER in which ground and roads go
                 under, and the scenario text on screen now says which of the two it is.
                 THE CHANNELS START DRY. No surviving gauge gives the Trishuli's flow that morning, and this model
                 cannot hold a river that falls 167 m across the domain at a steady level without inventing a
                 baseflow; an invented one would read as measurement. Every drop on screen is the modelled surge.
                 NO CASUALTY FIGURES appear anywhere in the app. The toll was still rising weeks later (NDRRMA, via
                 reporting: 1,114 on 2 September, 1,252 on 3-4 September, 1,386 on 12 September, with thousands
                 missing) and a simulation cannot produce such a number. It is recorded here, with its dates and its
                 authority, and nowhere else.
                 Context in the scenario text - the Betrawati bridge on the Pasang Lhamu Highway carrying the only
                 road into Rasuwa district, and the SMS alerts that went out minutes before the water - is from
                 ICIMOD (as above), the Kathmandu Post (29 August and 13 September 2026: washed-out bridges, 41
                 bridges and 40 km of highway lost, the Bailey bridge for the Phalakhu Khola) and Rising Nepal
                 (risingnepaldaily.com/news/54805, the concrete bridge at the Rasuwa-Nuwakot border).
                 SHELTERS are TWO real, named places, each spelled and positioned as its own node in the OSM
                 extracts this preset ships with, and each snapped by the bake to a high road node within 200 m:
                   * Shree Sundaradevi Pra Vi  node 5063441826  27.9762731 N, 85.1769728 E  -> shelter at 715 m,
                     80 m above the channel surface beside it
                   * Shree Sivalaya Ni Ma.V    node 4969611375  27.9835489 N, 85.1913123 E  -> shelter at 929 m,
                     276 m above the channel surface beside it
                 It said THREE until the September 2026 audit, and the third was wrong twice over. "Kalika Community
                 Hospital" was pinned to [85.18091, 28.02053], which is an untagged vertex of OSM way 443766738 (a
                 stream) 3.5 km north of the hospital and 753 m below it - a real hospital's name, on screen and in
                 the routing text, over a stream bank. The hospital's own node (4407834159, 27.9938874 N,
                 85.2034632 E, addr:city Kalikasthan) is inside the domain but stands at 1,520 m on this DEM, 919 m
                 above the valley floor on the Kalikasthan ridge; the research brief calls that "on the ridge - a long
                 climb, not a walk" and warns against a UI that implies the ridge tops are minutes away. Moving the
                 pin there would have corrected the name and told a worse lie - a 900 m climb offered as an
                 evacuation destination with a travel time beside it - so the entry is gone and the set is two.
                 Shree Sivalaya's name was also corrected here: OSM spells it "Ni Ma.V", not "Ni Ma Vi".
                 Shree Neelkanta Higher Sec School (node 4706138525) is not a shelter either, and NOT because it is
                 too low: it stands 706.4 m, 69 m clear of the channel beside it, comfortably over the 33 m this
                 preset requires. It is out because this bake's road graph has no node within its 200 m search radius
                 (the nearest three are 260 m away), so the shelter would sit off the road network, where the router
                 cannot reach it. The number this file used to give for it - "the highest road node within 200 m
                 stands 31.3 m above the channel, short of 33 m" - measured a road node 775 m away from the school,
                 beside the Trishuli rather than the Salankhu: the same class of error as the Kalika pin.
                 Shree Ramchandra Ni Ma Vi, 15 m above the channel, was never a candidate.
                 Every shelter name shipped is pinned to its OSM node id and coordinates by tests/data/presets.test.ts,
                 so a name can no longer drift away from the place it names.

Place names used for shelters and camera framing are ordinary geographic names; no third-party place database is
redistributed here. For the Nepal preset the two shelter names are read out of the OSM extracts listed above, and
that preset's `attribution` already carries the ODbL credit those extracts require.

Please credit the agencies when reusing the data: "Elevation: USGS 3DEP · Imagery: USDA NAIP via USGS The National
Map · Roads: U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line" (also stored as `attribution` in each meta.json). For the Nepal preset the
required credit is different and is likewise stored in its meta.json: "Elevation: Copernicus DEM GLO-30 (© DLR e.V.
2010–2014 / © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH, ESA), bare-earth filtered · Terrain shading: no aerial imagery
(hypsometric tint) · Roads © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL)".

Live areas loaded in the app are fetched at runtime and never stored in the repository. They use Esri World Imagery
(© Esri and its imagery providers, under Esri's terms) and, as fallbacks, Mapzen Terrarium elevation tiles (AWS Open
Data) and OpenStreetMap roads (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL).
