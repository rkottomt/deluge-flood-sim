U.S. dam index — source and terms
=================================

nid-index.bin   55,276 dams of the U.S. National Inventory of Dams: id, name, state, location, NID height, hydraulic height,
                NID (maximum) and normal storage, surface area, maximum discharge, length, year completed, hazard
                potential, primary owner type and primary dam type. Gzip-compressed binary (format: src/dams/nidIndex.ts),
                1.32 MB (3.45 MB uncompressed).

SOURCE          National Inventory of Dams (NID), U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, with the Federal Emergency Management
                Agency and the state and federal dam-safety agencies that report to it. National export, GeoPackage, public
                fields: https://nid.sec.usace.army.mil/api/nation/gpkg (downloaded October 2026; 92,625 records).

TERMS           The NID's public fields are public information published by USACE for download; some fields (for example
                condition assessments of certain federal dams, Emergency Action Plan details and inundation maps) are
                restricted and are not in the public export, nor here. Cite "U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, National
                Inventory of Dams". The index is a convenience copy: the NID itself is authoritative and updated
                continuously.

WHAT WAS KEPT   Dams with NID storage >= 1,000 acre-ft, or NID height >= 6 m (19.7 ft) with storage >= 50 acre-ft:
                55,887 records. Structures that share one NID id (a reservoir's main dam and its saddle dams) are folded
                into the tallest (609 folded), giving 55,278; the app's decoder refuses 2 more (one with no height, one
                with an implausible storage), leaving 55,276. Units are converted on use (ft -> m, acre-ft -> m3,
                ft3/s -> m3/s). A normal storage the NID reports as 0 is kept as 0 (1,128 dry dams: flood-control
                detention dams, tailings and debris basins with no permanent pool), apart from "not reported".

HOW IT WAS MADE scripts/bake-nid.ts (Node 22.13+, reads the GeoPackage with node:sqlite):
                    node --import tsx scripts/bake-nid.ts --download --cache=<dir>
                    node --import tsx scripts/bake-nid.ts --cache=<dir>

WHY NOT LIVE    The NID API answers a browser page with no Access-Control-Allow-Origin header (checked October 2026: a
                GET with an Origin header returns 200 with Access-Control-Allow-Credentials but no allowed origin, and the
                CSV export returns 403), so a page cannot read it. The baked copy also keeps the content-security policy
                free of a new endpoint and works offline.
